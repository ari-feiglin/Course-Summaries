\subsection{Separated and proper morphisms}

\subsubsection{Separated morphisms and varieties}

\bdefn

    Let $P$ be a property of morphisms.
    Define the property $P\delta$ ($P$-delta) to be the class of morphisms $\pi\colon X\to Y$ whose
    diagonal $\delta_\pi\colon X\to X\times_YX$ lies in $P$.

\edefn

\bthrm[name={cancellationtheorem}, title={The Cancellation Theorem}]

    Let $P$ be a class of morphisms preserved by base change and composition.
    Suppose we have a commutative diagram
    \diagram{
        X&&Y\cr
        &Z
    }{
        \diagarrow{from={1,1}, to={1,3}, text=\pi, y distance=.1cm}
        \diagarrow{from={1,1}, to={2,2}, text={\tau\in P}, x distance=-.15cm}
        \diagarrow{from={1,3}, to={2,2}, text={\rho\in P\delta}, x distance=.15cm}
    }
    If $\tau\in P$ and $\rho\in P\delta$ then $\pi\in P$.

\ethrm

\bproof

    Consider the diagram
    \diagram{
        X&X\times_YY&X\times_ZY&Z\times_ZY&Y\cr
        Y&Y\times_ZY&&X&Z
    }{
        \diagarrow{from={1,1}, to={1,2}, ds, right cap=-}
        \diagarrow{from={1,4}, to={1,5}, ds, right cap=-}
        \diagarrow{from={1,2}, to={1,3}}
        \diagarrow{from={1,3}, to={1,4}}
        \diagarrow{from={1,2}, to={2,1}}
        \diagarrow{from={1,3}, to={2,2}}
        \diagarrow{from={1,3}, to={2,4}}
        \diagarrow{from={1,4}, to={2,5}}
        \diagarrow{from={2,1}, to={2,2}, text=P, y distance=.15cm}
        \diagarrow{from={2,4}, to={2,5}, text=P, y distance=.15cm}
    }
    The squares are pullbacks, so the top arrows are in $P$, thus $X\to Y$ is.
    \qed

\eproof

\bprop

    If $P$ is a reasonable class of morphisms, so too is $P\delta$.

\eprop

\bproof

    First we show that $P\delta$ is closed under composition.
    If $X\to Y\to Z$ are morphisms such that $X\to X\times_YX$ and $Y\to Y\times_ZY$ are in $P$ then
    consider the diagram
    \diagram{
        X&X\times_YX&X\times_ZX\cr
        &Y&Y\times_ZY
    }{
        \diagarrow{from={1,1}, to={1,2}, text=P, y distance=.15cm}
        \diagarrow{from={2,2}, to={2,3}, text=P, y distance=.15cm}
        \diagarrow{from={1,2}, to={1,3}}
        \diagarrow{from={1,2}, to={2,2}}
        \diagarrow{from={1,3}, to={2,3}}
    }
    The square is a pullback so the top arrows are in $P$, thus $X\to X\times_ZX$ is.

    To show that $P\delta$ is closed under base change we must show that if $X\to Y$ is in $P\delta$ and
    $Z\to Y$ is a morphism, then $X\times_YZ\to Z$ is in $P\delta$.
    This boils down to showing if $X\to X\times_YX$ is in $P$ then $X\times_YZ\to X\times_YX\times_YZ$ is
    in $P$.
    This is because reasonable classes are closed under products.

    Suppose we have a cover $U_i$ of $Y$ such that $V_i\to U_i$ is in $P\delta$ where $V_i$ is the preimage
    of $U_i$.
    So we have a pullback diagram
    \diagram{
        V_i&U_i\cr
        X&Y
    }{
        \diagarrow{from={1,1}, to={1,2}, text=P\delta, y distance=.15cm}
        \diagarrow{from={1,1}, to={2,1}, left cap={(}}
        \diagarrow{from={1,2}, to={2,2}, left cap={(}}
        \diagarrow{from={2,1}, to={2,2}}
    }
    This means that the following diagram is a pullback as well
    \diagram{
        V_i&V_i\times_{U_i}V_i\cr
        X&X\times_YX
    }{
        \diagarrow{from={1,1}, to={1,2}, text=P, y distance=.15cm}
        \diagarrow{from={1,1}, to={2,1}, left cap={(}}
        \diagarrow{from={1,2}, to={2,2}, left cap={(}}
        \diagarrow{from={2,1}, to={2,2}}
    }
    And the $V_i\times_{U_i}V_i$ cover $X\times_YX$, so because $P$ is local on target so is $P\delta$.
    \qed

\eproof

\bexam

    Recall that $\pi\colon X\to Y$ is a monomorphism iff $\delta_\pi\colon X\to X\times_YX$ is an isomorphism.
    Because the property of being an isomorphism is reasonable, and its diagonal property is the property
    of being a monomorphism, it follows that being a monomorphism is reasonable.

\eexam

\bprop

    A morphism $\pi\colon X\to Y$ is quasiseparated iff the diagonal $\delta_\pi\colon X\to X\times_YX$
    is quasicompact.

\eprop

\bproof

    Suppose $\delta_\pi$ is quasicompact, then let $\spec A\subseteq Y$ be affine open.
    We want to show that if $U,V\subseteq X$ are affine open with $\pi(U),\pi(V)\subseteq\spec A$ then
    $U\cap V$ is compact.
    Because being quasicompact is reasonable, $U\cap V\to U\times_{\spec A}V$ is quasicompact.
    The righthand side is an affine scheme and thus compact, so its preimage $U\cap V$ is.
    Thus $\pi$ is quasiseparated.

    Conversely if $\pi$ is quasiseparated, then we may cover $X\times_YX$ with an affine cover of the form
    $U\times_WV$ where $\pi(U),\pi(V)\subseteq W$.
    On this target $\delta_\pi$ restricts to $U\cap V\to U\times_WV$.
    Because $\pi$ is quasiseparated, $U\cap V$ is quasicompact, and thus this is a morphism between
    quasicompact spaces and is thus quasicompact.
    \qed

\eproof

\bcoro

    Quasiseparated morphisms are reasonable.

\ecoro

\bprop[name={diagonallc}]

    Let $\pi\colon X\to Y$ be a morphism of schemes, and let $\delta_\pi$ be its diagonal.
    \benum
        \item If $X$ and $Y$ are affine, then $\delta_\pi$ is a closed embedding.
        \item In general $\delta_\pi$ is a locally closed embedding.
    \eenum

\eprop

\bproof

    If $X=\spec A,Y=\spec B$, then $X\times_YX=\spec A\otimes_BA$ and $A\otimes_BA\to A$ given by
    $a_1\otimes a_2\mapsto a_1a_2$ is surjective, proving (1).

    Cover $Y$ with affine opens $V_i$ and cover the preimage of $V_i$ in $X$ with affine opens $U_{ij}$.
    Then $U_{ij}\times_{V_i}U_{ij}$ is an affine open subscheme of $X\times_YX$, and they cover the image
    of $\delta_\pi$.
    Now $\delta_\pi^{-1}(U_{ij}\times_{V_i}U_{ij})=U_{ij}$ and $\delta_\pi$ restricts to a closed
    embedding $U_{ij}\to U_{ij}\times_{V_i}U_{ij}$ by (1), proving (2).

    Note that these open subsets may not cover $X\times_YX$, so $\delta_\pi$ is not necessarily a closed
    embedding.
    It is just a closed embedding into the union of these affine opens.
    \qed

\eproof

\bdefn

    A morphism $\pi\colon X\to Y$ is {\emphcolor separated} if $\delta_\pi\colon X\to X\times_YX$ is a
    closed embedding.
    An $A$-scheme is {\emphcolor separated} if the structure morphism $X\to\spec A$ is separated.

    By the above proposition and \refmath[proposition]{lcembcl}, $\pi$ is separated iff the
    $\Delta_\pi=\im\delta_\pi$ is closed in $X\times_YX$; this is purely topological.

\edefn

\bremark

    As closed embeddings are quasicompact (they are affine), a separated morphism is quasiseparated.

\eremark

\bprop

    Separated morphisms are reasonable.

\eprop

\bproof

    They are the diagonal (delta) property of being a closed embedding.
    \qed

\eproof

\bprop

    Affine morphisms are separated.

\eprop

\bproof

    By \refmath[proposition]{diagonallc} and separated morphisms being reasonable.
    \qed

\eproof

\bprop

    An $A$-scheme $X$ is separated iff it is separated as a $\bZ$-scheme.

\eprop

\bproof

    If $X$ is separated over $A$, then $X\to\spec A\to\spec\bZ$ is separated because affine morphisms are
    separated.
    Conversely if this composition is separated, then consider the diagram
    \diagram{
        X&&\spec A\cr
        &\spec\bZ
    }{
        \diagarrow{from={1,1}, to={1,3}}
        \diagarrow{from={1,1}, to={2,2}}
        \diagarrow{from={1,3}, to={2,2}}
    }
    Then $A\to\spec\bZ$ has the diagonal property of being separated (the diagonal is an affine morphism
    and thus separated), and $X\to\spec\bZ$ is separated by assumption.
    By \refmath{cancellationtheorem}, $X\to\spec A$ is separated.
    \qed

\eproof

We can generalize this result somewhat.

\bprop

    Consider the composition $\tau=X\xtos\pi Y\xtos\rho Z$.
    \benum
        \item If $\tau$ is separated (resp.\ locally closed, locally of finite type, quasiseparated),
        then so is $\pi$.
        \item If $\tau$ is quasicompact and $Y$ Noetherian, then $\pi$ is quasicompact.
    \eenum

\eprop

\bproof

    We appeal to \refmath{cancellationtheorem} and consider the diagram
    \diagram{
        X&&Y\cr
        &Z
    }{
        \diagarrow{from={1,1}, to={1,3}, text=\pi, y distance=.1cm}
        \diagarrow{from={1,1}, to={2,2}, text={\tau}, x distance=-.15cm}
        \diagarrow{from={1,3}, to={2,2}, text={\rho}, x distance=.15cm}
    }
    We want to show that in each case, $\rho\in P\delta$.
    The diagonal is always locally closed and thus of locally finite type, so in those cases we are done.
    And the diagonal is also always separated, because the diagonal of the diagonal is an isomorphism
    which is closed.
    And $\delta_\rho$ is quasiseparated iff its diagonal is quasicompact; the diagonal of the diagonal is an
    isomorphism, so we are done.
    This shows (1).

    And if $Y$ is Noetherian then $Y\to Y\times_ZY$ is quasicompact as all maps out of a Noetherian space
    are, showing (2).
    \qed

\eproof

\bexam

    The morphism $\bP^n_A\to\spec A$ is separated.

    Cover $\bP^n_A\times_A\bP^n_A$ with open subsets of the form $U_i\times_AU_j$ where $U_i$ are the usual
    affine cover of $\bP^n_A$.
    We want to show that $U_i\cap U_j\to U_i\times_AU_j$ is closed.
    When $i=j$ we showed this in \refmath[proposition]{diagonallc}.
    For $i\neq j$,
    $$ U_i\times_AU_j = \spec A[x_{0/i},\dots,x_{n/i},y_{0/j},\dots,y_{n/j}]/(x_{i/i}-1,y_{j/j}-1) , $$
    and
    $$ U_i\cap U_j = \spec A[x_{0/i},\dots,x_{n/i},1/x_{j/i}]/(x_{i/i}-1) . $$
    The morphism
    $$ A[x_{0/i},\dots,x_{n/i},y_{0/j},\dots,y_{n/j}]/(x_{i/i}-1,y_{j/j}-1) \to
    \spec A[x_{0/i},\dots,x_{n/i},1/x_{j/i}]/(x_{i/i}-1) $$
    given by $x_{k/i}\mapsto x_{k/i}$ and $y_{k/j}\mapsto x_{k/i}/x_{j/i}$ is surjective and thus we are done.

\eexam

\bdefn

    A {\emphcolor variety} over a field $k$ (a {\emphcolor $k$-variety}) is a reduced, separated scheme
    of finite type over $k$.
    A {\emphcolor subvariety} of a $k$-variety $X$ is a reduced locally closed subscheme of $X$.

\edefn

If $Z\inj X$ is locally closed, then it is of finite type.
Thus $Z\inj X\to k$ is of finite type.
Furthermore $Z\inj X$ is separated (because the diagonal is an isomorphism), and thus so is $Z\to k$.
Thus subvarieties are themselves varieties.

Note that $k$-varieties are Noetherian spaces: the preimage of $k$ in $X$ is a finite union of affine schemes,
which are all Noetherian spaces.

In general a morphism of $k$-varieties are of finite type and separated.
This is because if $X\to Y$ is a morphism of varieties, then $X\to Y\to k$ is of finite type and separated,
and by the above proposition $X\to Y$ is as well.

\bprop

    \benum
        \item If $k$ is perfect, the product of $k$-varieties (the fibered product over $\spec k$) is a
        $k$-variety.
        \item If $k$ is algebraically closed, the product of irreducible $k$-varieties is an irreducible
        $k$-variety.
        \item If $k$ is algebraically closed, the product of connected $k$-varieties is connected.
    \eenum

\eprop

\bproof

    If $X,Y$ are $k$-varieties then $X\times_kY\to X\to k$ is separated and of finite type by base change
    and composition.
    For reducedness we need to show that if $A,B$ are reduced $k$-algebras, then $A\otimes_kB$
    is as well.
    This is an algebraic result which is true.

    For (2) need only show that the product is irreducible.
    To show a space is irreducible it is sufficient to find a cover of irreducible open subsets which all
    have nonempty intersection.
    Let $U_i$ be an affine open cover of $X$ and $V_j$ for $Y$.
    Then $U_i\times V_j$ cover $X$, and they are irreducible (if $k$ is separably closed then if
    $\spec A,\spec B$ are irreducible so too is $\spec A\otimes_kB$), and their intersections are as well.
    \qed

\eproof

\bprop

    Suppose $X$ is a separated $A$-scheme, then the intersection of affine open subsets of $X$ is affine open.

\eprop

\bproof

    First note that $\Delta\cap(U\times_AV)\cong U\cap V$.
    This is because cones of one form below are the same as cones of the second:

    \medskip
    \hbox to\hsize{\hfill\commsqed{S&U\times_AV\cr X&X\times_AX}{}{}{}{}%
        \hfill%
        \commsqed{S&U\cr V&X}{}{}{}{}%
    }
    \medskip

    We have that $U\cap V\cong\Delta\cap(U\times_AV)$.
    We know that $U\times_AV$ is affine, and $\Delta\cap(U\times_AV)$ is a closed subscheme of $U\times_AV$
    and thus affine.
    \qed

\eproof

\bcoro

    The intersection of anty two affine open subsets of $\spec A$ is affine.

\ecoro

\bproof

    Take $X=\spec A$ in the above proposition.
    \qed

\eproof

\bcoro[name={quasiprojseparated}]

    Every quasiprojective $A$-scheme is separated over $A$.
    In particular every reduced quasiprojective $k$-scheme is a $k$-variety.

\ecoro

\bproof

    If $X\to\spec A$ is a quasiprojective $A$-scheme, then the structure morphism factors as a locally closed
    embedding followed by the structure morphism $X\to\bP^n_A\to\spec A$.
    Locally closed embeddings are separated, and thus $X\to\spec A$ is.
    \qed

\eproof

\subsubsection{Proper morphisms}

\bdefn

    A morphism $\pi\colon X\to Y$ is {\emphcolor universally closed} if for every $Z\to Y$, the induced
    morphism $Z\times_YX\to Z$ is closed (as a morphism of topological spaces).

    A morphism is {\emphcolor proper} if it is separated, of finite type, and universally closed.
    If $X$ is an $A$-scheme, it is said to be {\emphcolor proper} if the structure map $X\to\spec A$ is.

\edefn

Being universally closed is reasonable.
It is closed under composition because if $X\to Y\to Z$ are universally closed and $W\to Z$ is a morphism,
then consider the composition:
$$ W\times_ZX \to W\times_ZY \to W $$
the right arrow is closed.
The left arrow is as well because it is equal to
$$ W\times_ZX = W\times_ZY\times_YX \to W\times_ZY $$
which is closed.
It is closed under base change because if $X\to Y$ is universally closed, then for $Z\to Y$,
$X\times_YZ\to Z$ is also universally closed as for $W\to Z$,
$$ X\times_YZ\times_ZW = X\times_YW \to W $$
is closed.
Finally it is local on target: if $V\subseteq X$ is mapped into $U\subseteq Y$ then consider the pullback
for $Z\to U$,
\diagram{
    V\times_UZ&Z\cr
    V&U\cr
    X&Y
}{
    \diagarrow{from={1,1}, to={1,2}}
    \diagarrow{from={2,1}, to={2,2}}
    \diagarrow{from={3,1}, to={3,2}}
    \diagarrow{from={1,1}, to={2,1}}
    \diagarrow{from={2,1}, to={3,1}}
    \diagarrow{from={1,2}, to={2,2}}
    \diagarrow{from={2,2}, to={3,2}}
}
So we see that $V\times_UZ\to Z$ is the pullback of $X\to Y$, and is thus universally closed (in particular
closed).
Now cover $Y$ with $U_i$ whose preimages in $X$ are $V_i$ and suppose $V_i\to U_i$ are locally closed.
Let $Z\to Y$ be a morphism and let $Z_i$ be the preimages of $U_i$.
Then $Z_i\times_{U_i}V_i\to Z_i$ are closed, and they glue to give $Z\times_YX\to Z$.
Being a closed map is local on the target so we are done.

Because each condition in being proper is then reasonable, being proper is reasonable.

\bprop

    The classes of universally closed and proper morphisms are reasonable.

\eprop

\bprop

    Finite morphisms are proper.
    In particular closed embeddings are proper.

\eprop

\bproof

    Finite morphisms are by definition affine and thus separated, and finite morphisms are of finite type.
    So we need only show that finite morphisms are universally closed.
    Recall that finite morphisms are reasonable, so they remain finite after base change, and finite
    morphisms are also closed.
    \qed

\eproof

\bthrm

    Projective $A$-schemes are proper over $A$.

\ethrm

\bproof

    A projective $A$-scheme $X$ is a closed subscheme of $\bP^n_A$.
    Because closed embeddings are proper, it is sufficient to show that $\bP^n_A$ is proper.
    Clearly $\bP^n_A\to\spec A$ is of finite type, and we showed it is separated by
    \refmath[corollary]{quasiprojseparated}.
    So it remains to show that it is universally closed.

    Because $\bP^n_A=\bP^n_\bZ\times_\bZ\spec A$, it is sufficient to show that $\bP^n_\bZ\to\spec\bZ$
    is universally closed.
    That is, we want to show that $\bP^n_X=\bP^n_\bZ\times_\bZ X\to X$ is closed for all schemes $X$.
    Being closed is local on the target, so it is sufficient to show this for affine $X=\spec B$.
    And $\bP^n_B\to\spec B$ is indeed closed (this is the fundamental theorem of elimination theory and is
    non-trivial).
    \qed

\eproof

\subsubsection{Valuation rings and criteria for separatedness and properness}

\bdefn

    Let $k$ be a field and $G$ a linearly ordered Abelian group.
    A {\emphcolor valuation} of $k$ with values in $G$ is a map $v\colon k^\times\to G$ such that for all
    nonzero $x,y\in k$,
    $$ (1)\ v(xy) = v(x) + v(y),\qquad (2)\ v(x+y) \geq \min(v(x),v(y)) , $$
    where we set $v(0)=\infty$.

\edefn

If $v\colon k^\times\to G$ is a valuation, then $A=\set{x\in k}[v(x)\geq0]\cup0$ is a subring of $k$.
Indeed, because $v$ is a group morphism we have $v(1)=0$ so it lies in $A$, the sum of elements of $A$
clearly lie in $A$, so does the product, and $2v(-1)=v(1)=0$ so that $v(-1)=0$ and thus $-1\in A$.
So $A$ is a ring, called the {\emphcolor valuation ring of $v$} (or {\it a\/} valuation ring of $k$).

Define $\m=\set{x\in k}[v(x)>0]\cup0$; this is a proper ideal of $A$.
And if $v(x)=0$ then $x^{-1}\in k$ is in $A$ because $0=v(xx^{-1})=v(x)+v(x^{-1})$ so $v(x^{-1})=0$.
Thus $x\notin\m$ iff $x$ is invertible, so $(A,\m)$ is a local ring.

\bdefn

    A {\emphcolor valuation ring} is an integral domain which is the valuation ring of some valuation on
    its field of fractions.

    If $\ell\leq k$ is a subfield and $v(x)=0$ for $x\in\ell$, then $v$ is a valuation of $k/\ell$.
    And its valuation ring is a valuation ring of $k/\ell$.

\edefn

\bdefn

    Suppose $A,B$ are local rings, then $B$ {\emphcolor dominates} $A$ if $A\subseteq B$ and inclusion forms
    a local map $A\inj B$; i.e.\ $\m_A=\m_B\cap A$.

\edefn

\bthrm

    Let $k$ be a field, then a local ring $A\subseteq k$ is a valuation ring of $k$ iff it is a maximal
    local subring of $k$ with respect to domination.
    Furthermore every local subring of $k$ is dominated by some valuation ring of $k$.

\ethrm

\bdefn

    A valuation ring is {\emphcolor discrete} if its value group $G$ is the integers.
    The corresponding valuation ring is called a {\emphcolor discrete valuation ring} ({\emphcolor DVR}).

\edefn

\bthrm

    Let $A$ be a Noetherian local domain of dimension $1$, with maximal ideal $\m$.
    The following are equivalent.
    \benum
        \item $A$ is a DVR,
        \item $A$ is normal (integrally closed),
        \item $A$ is a regular local ring,
        \item $\m$ is a principal ideal.
    \eenum

\ethrm

\bnote

    A regular local ring is a Noetherian local ring which satisfies any of the following equivalent properties:
    \benum
        \item The Krull dimension of $A$ is equal to the minimal number of generators of its maximal ideal.
        This is well-defined because the ring is Noetherian and so $\m$ is finitely generated.
        \item The Krull dimension of $A$ is equal to $\dim_k\m/\m^2$ where $k=A/\m$ is $A$'s residue field.
    \eenum
    Clearly the first property here is equivalent to (4) above as $A$ has dimension $1$.

\enote

\bdefn

    A {\emphcolor Dedekind domain} is an integrally closed Noetherian domain of dimension one.

\edefn

Thus the localization of a Dedekind domain at a nonzero prime ideal is a DVR.
